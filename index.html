Twelve people have walked on the Moon

All of them on Apollo 11 to 17, between 1969 and 1972. Nobody went back for 54 years, until Artemis II flew four astronauts around the Moon in April 2026: 695,081 miles in 9 days, 1 hour and 32 minutes.

Artemis III (late 2027) will practise docking with the new landers in Earth orbit. The first Artemis landing is now Artemis IV, planned for 2028.

How getting to orbit works

7.8 km/sspeed needed to stay in low orbit, about 28,000 km/h
11.2 km/sescape velocity from Earth’s surface
~100 kmthe Kármán line, a common definition of where space starts
~90%of a rocket’s liftoff mass is propellant

Getting to orbit is mostly about going sideways very fast. A rocket climbs straight up to get out of the thick lower atmosphere, then tips over in what’s called the gravity turn and spends most of its fuel building horizontal speed. Once it moves sideways fast enough, it keeps falling around Earth instead of back onto it. The rocket equation, Δv = Isp · g₀ · ln(m₀/m_f), shows why rockets use stages: throwing away empty tanks makes what’s left far more efficient.
